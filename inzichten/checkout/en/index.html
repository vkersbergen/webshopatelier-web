How to improve your mobile checkout

By WebshopAtelier · Updated 21 November 2026

A shopper taps “Checkout” and never comes back. Perhaps that’s the first time she sees the delivery charge. Or perhaps the payment went through and only the tracking failed. Follow a single order on your own phone, from the product page through to confirmation. This will tell you whether to investigate checkout or reporting first.

In this article
  • At which step do shoppers drop out?
  • Place a test order using one hand
  • Check the result in both your store and your report

At which step do shoppers drop out?

For each device type, compare the number of sessions that reach checkout with the number that end in a purchase. Use the same time period, traffic source and report definition. Adding to a basket happens earlier than starting checkout. Someone using a basket as a wishlist tells you little about how well the payment page works. See Shopify’s definitions of the funnel stages.

Worked example. On mobile, 120 sessions reach checkout and 48 complete it, giving a 40% completion rate. On desktop, 42 out of 70 complete it, or 60%. Investigate the mobile journey first. Check whether the two groups include comparable products, countries and traffic sources before concluding that mobile checkout is at fault.

Place a test order using one hand

  1. Before checkout. Can you find the delivery costs, delivery times and any free-shipping threshold? Change the quantity and check whether the total updates immediately.
  2. While filling in details. Order as a new customer. Can you find guest checkout and use autofill? Deliberately enter an invalid postcode. Check that the error explains what to fix and preserves the rest of your address.
  3. During payment. In test mode, try a successful payment, a declined payment and an abandoned payment. When returning from the payment app, check that shoppers see the right status and can retry a failed payment. Record which order appears in your store admin for each test.

Baymard’s research identifies unexpected extra costs and mandatory account creation among the reasons people abandon checkout. Use those findings to focus your tests. Look at your own customers and order records to decide what to fix first. Read Baymard’s checkout research.

Check the result in both your store and your report

Find the test order in your store admin. Check that your analytics recorded the start of checkout and that the recorded purchase has the correct order number. Refreshing the confirmation page should never record a second purchase. Google documents the ecommerce events available in GA4.

Money received and one order in the store, but two purchases in your report? Fix the measurement first. If the order is missing from the store too, record the device, payment method and exact step that failed. A repeatable case gives a checkout review a clear starting point. If hardly anyone gets that far, inspect the steps before checkout.
