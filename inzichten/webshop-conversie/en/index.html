How to improve your store’s conversion rate: where to start

By WebshopAtelier · Updated 21 November 2026

Want more sales from the same number of visitors? Find the first step where comparable shoppers drop out more often than before. A conversion problem may start with the ad, the product selection or the final payment. Each calls for a different fix.

In this article

  • Calculate conversion per session
  • What does each stage tell you?
  • Choose one worthwhile test
  • What should improve after the test?

Calculate conversion per session

Divide the number of sessions with a purchase by the total number of store sessions and multiply by 100. If 60 out of 3,000 sessions include a purchase, your conversion rate is 2%. One person can generate several sessions. Document which measure you use and take both figures from the same source. See what Shopify counts in its behaviour reports.

What does each stage tell you?

  • People visit but few view a product. Open the ad on your phone and follow the link. Do the product, price and promise match what shoppers find on the landing page?
  • They view a product but don’t add it to their basket. For your most popular product, look for questions about size, material, delivery or use. Address the most frequent concern next to the buy button. Check that the variant shoppers want is in stock.
  • They fill their basket but don’t pay. Test delivery, charges and payment methods. An extra product photo cannot fix a declined payment. Work through the mobile checkout tests.

Choose one worthwhile test

Worked example. Out of 3,000 sessions, 300 add an item, 150 reach checkout and 60 buy. The overall conversion rate is 2%, while 40% of started checkouts are completed. If customers regularly ask about delivery costs, showing the charge in the basket is a focused test. An increase in checkout starts alone is a poor reason to keep a change if paid orders don’t increase.

Compare mobile with mobile. Break down paid traffic, search traffic and returning visitors. A campaign that attracts many new visitors can lower your overall conversion rate while orders rise. With little traffic, repeated customer questions, recordings collected with consent and reproducible errors often give you a better starting point than a short A/B test.

What should improve after the test?

Suppose a discount produces three orders per hundred sessions at €8 contribution each. Before the discount you had two orders at €15 each. Assuming one order per purchasing session, conversion rises from 2% to 3%, yet contribution per hundred sessions falls from €30 to €24. Check both figures before keeping the change. This is the type of decision a store conversion review should help you make.
