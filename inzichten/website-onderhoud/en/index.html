Website maintenance: what should you check, and how often?

By WebshopAtelier · Updated 21 November 2026

Website maintenance keeps the path from first visit to enquiry or order working. A contact page can load perfectly even when its messages never arrive. For each important journey, agree who will test it, what a successful test looks like and who will fix a failure.

What should you check after every change?

  • A service website. Open the mobile menu, choose a service and send a clearly labelled test enquiry. Check the receiving inbox and the reply-to address.
  • An online store. Select a variant, check stock and shipping costs, then go through payment and order confirmation. On Shopify, follow the instructions for placing a test order.
  • A migration or a new page structure. Open old links that attracted significant traffic. Check that each one takes visitors to the right new destination.

What maintenance does your platform need?

If you run your own WordPress site, you need a restorable backup of both the files and the database. Try restoring it in a separate test environment. WordPress explains what a full backup contains. On a hosted platform, your responsibilities shift towards your theme, apps, access permissions and the content you manage. Ask the provider exactly what it can restore.

How should you agree on maintenance?

A brief monthly test of the main journeys, followed by another check after every change, is a practical starting point. Test more often if you receive a high volume of daily orders or enquiries. Agree who gets alerts, when support is available and who can authorise a restore.

Ask for a simple log showing what was tested, what went wrong, who fixed it and when the retest passed. This shows what your maintenance budget pays for. Explore our maintenance and development services or read how to compare build and running costs.
